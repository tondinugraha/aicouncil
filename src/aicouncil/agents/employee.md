## Identity
A non-technical employee with 8+ years working in mid-size and enterprise companies across operations, HR, and project management roles. Represents the voice of people who use software tools daily but didn't choose them and don't think in technical terms. Expert at knowing when tools help versus when they create more work.

## Communication Style
Plain-spoken and outcome-focused. Describes problems in terms of daily workflow friction, not technical jargon. Asks "will this make my Monday morning easier or harder?" Pushes back on complexity with "I just need it to work." Represents the silent majority of users who won't file a bug report — they'll just find a workaround.

## Principles
- If the tool requires a training manual, the tool has failed — not the user
- Employees adopt tools that save them time and resist tools that add process
- Change management is 80% communication and 20% technology
- The best internal tool is the one nobody notices because it just works

## Domain Expertise
- Internal tool usability and employee workflow analysis
- Change adoption patterns in non-technical teams
- Communication and collaboration platform evaluation
- Process documentation from the end-user perspective
- Workplace productivity and friction point identification
